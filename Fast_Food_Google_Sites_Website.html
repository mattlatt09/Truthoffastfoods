What's Really in Fast Food?

A closer look at popular fast-food meals, unfamiliar ingredients, nutrition, possible health effects, and simple alternatives.

Why This Matters

Fast food is convenient, affordable, and popular. The important question is not whether one meal is automatically “good” or “bad,” but whether we understand what we are eating and how it fits into our overall diet.

Current data
30.1%

of U.S. children and adolescents ages 2–19 consumed fast food on a given day during August 2021–August 2023.

Teenagers
14.6%

of adolescents ages 12–19 got this percentage of their daily calories from fast food on average during the same period.

The bigger picture

CDC research connects fast-food consumption with higher calorie intake and poorer diet quality among children and adolescents.

Why understand what you're digesting?
Food supplies energy and nutrients that your body uses for growth, movement, concentration, and everyday functions. Reading ingredient lists and nutrition facts helps you recognize sodium, added sugars, fats, preservatives, and other ingredients instead of relying only on advertising or appearance.

3 Ingredients People May Not Recognize

These ingredients sound complicated, but a complicated name does not automatically mean an ingredient is dangerous. The important part is understanding what the ingredient does and how the overall food fits into a diet.

01

Dimethylpolysiloxane

What it does: Chick-fil-A lists it in the oil used for its chicken and waffle fries as an anti-foaming agent.

Why it is there: It helps control foam during cooking.

The FDA lists dimethylpolysiloxane among substances with authorized food uses.

02

Calcium Propionate

What it does: Chick-fil-A lists it as a preservative in its bun.

Why it is there: Calcium propionate is used as an antimicrobial agent to help prevent spoilage.

The FDA lists calcium propionate as a food ingredient with authorized uses.

03

Sodium Benzoate

What it does: Sodium benzoate is a preservative used in some foods and beverages.

Why it is there: It can help control microorganisms and maintain product quality.

Always check the specific product's ingredient list because ingredients differ between foods and restaurants.

Important distinction: Food additives can have useful purposes. The FDA explains that ingredients may be added to maintain safety and freshness, improve texture, control color, or provide other technical functions. “Chemical-sounding” is not the same thing as “unsafe.”

What Can Become a Problem?

The main concern is usually the overall nutrition of the meal and how often foods high in certain nutrients are eaten, not one ingredient by itself.

3 Healthier Alternatives

These are simple meals that can still feel filling and convenient while giving you more control over ingredients, portions, sodium, and added sugar.

Meal 01

Grilled Chicken Bowl

Grilled chicken + brown rice + black beans + lettuce + tomatoes + avocado.

Why it works: It combines protein, whole grains, vegetables, and fiber-rich beans.

Meal 02

Turkey & Veggie Wrap

Whole-grain tortilla + turkey + lettuce + tomato + cucumber + a light yogurt-based sauce.

Why it works: You can control how much sauce and salt you add while including vegetables and whole grains.

Meal 03

Homemade Burger Plate

Lean beef or turkey burger + whole-grain bun + lettuce and tomato + oven-baked potato wedges + fruit.

Why it works: Making it at home lets you choose the meat, amount of salt, toppings, cooking oil, and side.

What Should We Take Away?

Read the label

Nutrition Facts show amounts of calories, sodium, added sugar, saturated fat, and other nutrients.

Look beyond the name

A food can sound simple while still containing many ingredients used for flavor, texture, preservation, or cooking.

Think about the whole meal

A burger, fries, sauce, and sugary drink can add up quickly. Looking at the entire meal gives a more accurate picture.

Don't fear ingredients

Unfamiliar names are not automatically harmful. Learn what they do and use reliable sources before making a judgment.
